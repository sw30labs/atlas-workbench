# Acknowledgments

Atlas Workbench exists at the intersection of operational-risk practice,
uncertainty research, and open-source agent engineering. Acknowledgment means
influence or technical reliance; it does not imply affiliation, approval, or
endorsement.

## Intellectual influences

Nassim Nicholas Taleb’s work helped frame the project’s interest in uncertainty,
fragility, tail events, and preparedness:

- [*The Black Swan: The Impact of the Highly Improbable*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/176226/the-black-swan-second-edition-by-nassim-nicholas-taleb/)
- [*Antifragile: Things That Gain from Disorder*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/176227/antifragile-by-nassim-nicholas-taleb/)

The project applies those broad ideas to its own research workflow. It does not
attempt to imitate Taleb, write in his style, or create a Taleb persona.

The radar extension is informed by general horizon-scanning and
strategic-imagination practice, including the publicly described work of
[TomorrowToday Global](https://tomorrowtodayglobal.com/imaginethefuture/).
Atlas Workbench uses independently authored terminology, software, prompts,
and synthetic examples. It does not incorporate TomorrowToday scenarios,
toolkit content, or proprietary material. No affiliation or endorsement is
implied.

## Open-source foundations

- [LangGraph](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/overview) provides
  graph orchestration for the multi-phase workflow.
- [oMLX](https://github.com/jundot/omlx) can provide a local
  OpenAI-compatible inference endpoint.
- [DriftLab](https://github.com/sw30labs/driftlab-dgx) provides runtime trace
  and baseline-oriented governance concepts used by the integration.

The Python ecosystem and all direct dependencies are listed in
[THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). Their maintainers and
contributors make this project possible.
